Project 01 · ME101 Final · Feb – Apr 2026

Autonomous Cookie Icing Machine

SolidWorksC++FabricationDFMCircuits3D PrintingLaser Cutting
< 4 min icing cycle 3 icing colours 4-person team
Objective

Design, build, and program an autonomous machine that applies a user-designed multi-colour icing pattern to a cookie in under 4 minutes — using at least four 3D-printed or laser-cut parts and at least two VEX IQ motors and sensors.

My Contribution

I modelled the gantry and icing dispenser in SolidWorks and led most of the fabrication: 3D-printing motor casings and the gantry, and laser-cutting the acrylic racks, baseplate, and enclosure. Facing a motor shortage, I proposed moving the cookie baseplate instead of the nozzle — improving icing accuracy and reducing load on the upper motors. Iterative testing also led me to implement plunger logic that prevents failed dispensing from trapped air and icing pooling.

Outcome & Learning

The machine met every engineering specification. I learned how critical sensor calibration is for reliable autonomous operation — sensors are sensitive and rarely behave exactly as their datasheet promises.

Project 02 · ME100 Final · Oct – Dec 2025

Mechanical Thumb Toy — “Digit”

SolidWorksIterative Design3D PrintingFunctional TestingTechnical Reports
30 N grip force spec met < 1 s actuation 3 design iterations
Objective

Create a mechanically operated toy for the ME100 course final project, applying 3D modelling and machine shop skills through the engineering design process.

My Contribution

I owned several core mechanical components: the hand-mounting mechanism and the elastic extension mechanism that returns the thumb to neutral. My initial design used a thumb-slide and string for actuation — but testing showed this defeated Digit’s purpose as an extra finger, since it occupied the user’s own thumb. I transitioned to wrist-based actuation, freeing all five fingers while enabling controlled movement of Digit — the configuration we shipped.

Outcome & Learning

Digit met its 30 N grip-force spec with sub-second actuation, and the working prototype was kept by the professor as an example for future students. I learned to build independent mechanical motion paths tied to specific hand movements — preventing wrist rotation from accidentally triggering extension or retraction.

Project 04 · ME100 · Oct 2025

DIY Dynamometer

ExcelData AnalysisTechnical Report Writing
45% deviation quantified 2 scatter-plot analyses 5-person team
Objective

Design and build a dynamometer to measure the torque output of a TT motor, using a TETRIX® robotics set plus a switch, breadboard, jumper wires and power supply — with only research we found online to guide the design of either a rope or Prony brake.

My Contribution

We chose a rope brake for its lower part count, and I designed the final structure — a tall frame giving a wider measurement range than the table-hung alternative. I built the two key analyses in Excel with best-fit lines (elastic force vs. displacement, and torque vs. motor speed) and authored the report section on the torque-speed curve and sources of measurement inaccuracy.

Outcome & Learning

The team submitted a report evaluating our torque measurements against manufacturer values — quantifying a 45% deviation and identifying its likely sources. I learned how dynamometers work, how to translate research into a physical product, and how to read correlation from graphs.
