01 · COOKIE ICING MACHINE DWG NO. 02

Autonomous Cookie Icing Machine

ME101 Final · Feb – Apr 2026 · 4-person team

SolidWorksC++FabricationDFMCircuits3D PrintingLaser Cutting
  • < 4 min icing cycle
  • 3 icing colours
Objective

Design, build, and program an autonomous cookie icing machine capable of applying a user designed multi-colour icing pattern to the surface of a cookie in <240 secs. This was the final project for ME101 in my 1B term.

My Contribution

I modelled the gantry and icing dispenser in SolidWorks and led most of the fabrication including 3D printing motor casings and the gantry, and laser-cutting the acrylic racks, baseplate, and enclosure. Facing a motor shortage, I proposed moving the cookie baseplate instead of the nozzle which improved icing accuracy and reduced load on the upper motors. I conducted iterative testing throughout and used the results to implement plunger logic that prevents dispensing failures from trapped air and icing pooling.

Outcome & Learning

The machine met every engineering specification. I learned how critical sensor calibration is for reliable autonomous operation: sensors are sensitive and rarely behave exactly as their datasheet promises.

02 · MECHANICAL THUMB “DIGIT” DWG NO. 03

Mechanical Thumb Toy “Digit”

ME100 Final · Oct – Dec 2025 · 3 design iterations

SolidWorksIterative Design3D PrintingFunctional TestingTechnical Reports
  • 30 N grip force spec met
  • < 1 s actuation
Objective

Create a mechanically operated toy for the ME100 course final project, applying 3D modelling and machine shop skills through the engineering design process.

My Contribution

I owned several core mechanical components: the hand-mounting mechanism and the elastic extension mechanism that returns the thumb to neutral. My initial design used a thumb-slide and string for actuation but testing showed this defeated Digit’s purpose as an extra finger, since it occupied the user’s own thumb. I transitioned to wrist-based actuation, freeing all five fingers while enabling controlled movement of Digit, and this was the configuration we shipped.

Outcome & Learning

Digit met its 30 N grip-force spec with sub-second actuation, and the working prototype was kept by the professor as an example for future students. I learned to build independent mechanical motion paths tied to specific hand movements, preventing wrist rotation from accidentally triggering extension or retraction.

04 · DIY DYNAMOMETER DWG NO. 05

DIY Dynamometer

ME100 · Oct 2025 · 5-person team

ExcelData AnalysisTechnical Report Writing
  • 45% deviation quantified
  • 2 scatter-plot analyses
Objective

Design and build a dynamometer to measure the torque output of a TT motor, using a TETRIX® robotics set plus a switch, breadboard, jumper wires, and power supply. Online research on rope and Prony brakes guided the design.

My Contribution

We chose a rope brake for its lower part count, and I designed the final structure which was a tall frame giving a wider measurement range than the alternative which hung from the table. I also built the two key analyses in Excel with best-fit lines (elastic force vs. displacement, and torque vs. motor speed) and authored the report section on the torque-speed curve and sources of measurement inaccuracy.

Outcome & Learning

The team submitted a report evaluating our torque measurements against manufacturer values, quantifying a 45% deviation and identifying its likely sources. I learned how dynamometers work, how to translate research into a physical product, and how to read correlation from graphs.
